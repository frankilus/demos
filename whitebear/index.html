The famous one

Ask anyone in Flushing where the best wontons in New York are and the answer comes back as a number. No. 6 — Wontons with Hot Sauce — has been written up by the New York Times, Serious Eats and Gothamist, and it still comes out of the same little window, in the same plastic bowl, for about ten dollars.

The chili oil is the point: roasted, smoky, more fragrant than fiery, so the pork can still talk. Regulars scrape the bowl clean. First-timers are warned — order two if you're sharing.

EST.

A Flushing institution

There is no dining room to speak of, no décor to review, and no interest in either. White Bear is a window, a counter, and a couple who have been folding wontons behind it for years — Shandong-style dumplings and Sichuan heat, served to whoever is standing on Roosevelt Avenue.

Order anything else and the counter may gently steer you back to the No. 6. Take the hint. Then take the bowl to the little park around the corner like everyone else, or carry it home next to a bag of frozen ones for later.

Small place. Enormous reputation. That's the whole story, and it's enough.
